## Week Two:  The Structures of World History  (Aug 25-Aug 27)

In the first a series of context weeks, we are going to pull the camera very, very far back to look at the structures of world history, how mode of exchange, mode of production, custom, and culture share deeper structures even as they are specific to a given time and place. Pay close attention this week to Karatani's argument on how the organization of exchange both effects? What values do they represent? Can you imagine current examples of those modes still at work?

**Tuesday:** Defining Modes of Exchange

**Read:**

* Karatani, Kojin. 2014. *The Structures of World History: From Modes of Production to Modes of Exchange*. Trans. by Michael K. Bourdaghs. Duke University Press. "Introduction." Pp. 1-28.


**Thursday:** The Sedentary Revolution  

**Read:**

* Karatani, Kojin. 2014. *The Structures of World History: From Modes of Production to Modes of Exchange*. Trans. by Michael K. Bourdaghs. Duke University Press. "The Sedentary Revolution" and "The Gift and Magic." Pp. 35-56.
