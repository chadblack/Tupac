## Researching Like a Historian 

**DUE: OCTOBER 8th**

Historians don't only read and synthesize information. They also, of course, make new analyses of documents, bringing new questions, theories, connections, etc. to bear on the past. Evidence must be evaluated, sorted, analyzed in an ongoing process. In this assignment, you will demonstrate that you can do this clearly and in an interesting fashion.

In this assignment, you will practice reading a set of primary sources to understand the context they describe for the social order that produced the rebellion. Students will read, annotate, and synthesize the documents in Chapter IV: The Breakdown of Colonial Order. Students will then write an analysis of the tensions described in these documents. What can you know about the world you're describing, based solely on that set of sources? Spend some time reading through a each selection. Who is writing? What type of information is  included in the source? What words, phrases, references do you not know or understand in the source? Circle or highlight them. Read the content, but also beyond the content of the source.

Begin your essay with an argument-- an argument for why the source matters. But also, articulate what questions and limitations it forces you to confront in your own knowledge. **Do not include any additional resources or references except those assigned in class. If you use outside resources and cite them, you'll get a zero. If you use them and don't cite them, you'll be referred for plagiarism.**  

* Use prose, with well conceived paragraphs.  
* Between 1000-1500 words (include a word count)  
* State an argument in the first sentence of the paper; that's your thesis.  
* Include at least 3 quotes from the sources.  
* Organization should be clear, with transitions between paragraphs.  
*  Use footnotes for the three quotes and for any other idea or paraphrase you're referencing from the text. Footnote quotes immediate. Other information can be noted at the end of its paragraph. Use [The Chicago Manual of Style citation guide](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide/citation-guide-1.html).
