## Where'd That Come From? 

**DUE: November 6th**

Historians work with an enormous variety of information, integrating people, events, broad patterns, theoretical arguments, and more. In this assignment, you will work backwards from a finished piece of historical work to recreate the difficult syntheses at the root of historical research. 

For this assignment, students will us the following source, available on Canvas:

Garrett, David T. 2004. '"His Majesty's Most Loyal Vassals": The Indian Nobility and Túpac Amaru.' *Hispanic American Historical Review*. 84:4, pp. 575-617. 


You will take a deep dive into the footnotes/citations of this piece.

1. To start, you will write a précis and research memo for the  article, identifying its primary purpose and overall structure. 
2. Create a categorized bibliography of every source cited in the piece. You must choose your own categorization-- thematic, temporal, primary/secondary, etc. Explain why you divided the sources up the way that you did. 
3. See if you can locate copies of any of the sources. Very old books can often be found on [archive.org](https://archive.org),  [hathitrust.org](https://hathitrust.org), or [books.google.com](https://books.google.com). Other books or articles may be in our own library's collections. Primary sources from Spanish or Latin American Archives may be in digital collections of those archives-- it's hard to say. There is an extensive collection of primary sources in Spanish  on Tupac Amaru that has been published as a seven volume series titled *Nueva colección documental de la Independencia del Perú: La Rebelión de Túpac Amaru*, editions prepared by Carlos Daniel Valcarcel, with a prologue by Charles Walker. 
4. Pick a few of those sources and read through or interact with them. Compare your own experience with them to how they're used in your article/chapter. Clearly, the author is more capable in this task, but the point is to intellectually exercise and understand how research develops.  
5. Write up your experience with these sources following the categorized bibliography. What don't you know? Did the sources lead you to others along the web of signifiers?  

As with previous assignments, use prose with paragraphs for everything except the categorized bibliography itself. Use quotes are part of explaining your own experience with the sources you chose. Organization should be clear, with transitions between paragraphs. Continue to use the same citation style and formatting we've used thus far, including on your bibliography.
